# Business interview questions and structured answers

All project results are demonstration evidence. Hypothetical audiences below are case assumptions, not measured bank populations.

1. **How would you increase salary domiciliation?** Define first recurring salary receipt and persistence, validate eligibility/current ownership, inspect the readiness funnel, understand barriers, then randomize an appropriate offer against control. Measure incremental conversion and customer/cost guardrails before scale.
2. **How would you increase Travel Plan adoption?** Start with customer need and eligible nonowners, inspect offered-to-activated drop-off, compare suitable channels and avoid contacting existing owners. The simulated channel trial fails a guardrail; its positive conversion estimate is not a reason to scale.
3. **How would you evaluate a campaign?** Confirm delivery, population, mature window and contact denominator. Review conversion, costs, incremental contrast against randomized control, uncertainty and guardrails. Keep observational attribution separate.
4. **How identify high-value customers?** Agree the business meaning of value. This project uses a transparent current balance/salary/tenure/credit proxy with missingness flags, not profitability or CLV. Investigate robustness before targeting.
5. **Which KPIs matter?** Tie each to a decision: eligible conversion, salary domiciliation, digital period retention, product depth, service activation, NPS and incremental economics. State population, numerator, denominator and date/window.
6. **How increase engagement?** Identify meaningful activity and customer friction, distinguish dormant from low-frequency but satisfied users, propose useful onboarding/features, then test rather than rewarding empty logins.
7. **How measure satisfaction?** Combine survey response rate, NPS/satisfaction distribution, complaints and resolution. Check response bias and avoid treating a small responding segment as all customers.
8. **What if conversion falls?** First validate tracking, eligibility, maturity and mix. Then inspect delivery/open/click/conversion stages by channel and cohort, compare control, investigate product friction and pause if guardrails deteriorate.
9. **How prioritize opportunities?** Apply consent/eligibility, ownership, holdout and contact caps first. Rank remaining hypotheses by need, cautious evidence, value proxy, effort and customer risk; retain a no-action option.
10. **Why can CTR rise while conversion falls?** The message may attract attention without relevant intent, the landing flow may fail, or denominator/window mix may change. Inspect the entire mature funnel and randomized outcome.
11. **Is highest propensity the best target?** Not necessarily. Likely converters may convert without a campaign. Need incremental effect, costs and customer experience, not only response probability.
12. **How explain a weak model?** Show baseline, holdout sample and capacity metrics, check feature timing and labels, and keep rules where appropriate. Better evidence can be more valuable than another estimator.
13. **How interpret retention?** Define an entry cohort, meaningful activity, observable age period and reactivation policy. Digital period retention differs from account survival and confirmed churn.
14. **What makes an experiment trustworthy?** Predefined eligibility/outcome, stable randomization, no contamination, complete ITT data, SRM/exposure diagnostics, enough sample and guardrail-aware decisions.
15. **What is Primary Bank here?** A readiness proxy from relationship depth, payroll and engagement. The nested funnel is current-state association, not proof of a customer's historical progression.
16. **Would you scale the payroll result?** No. The simulated contrast is +9.20 percentage points but only 306/305 participants versus 569 planned per arm. Do not select a result purely because its adjusted p-value is small.
17. **How resolve a KPI disagreement?** Align the decision, owner, grain, eligible population, null policy and window. Reconcile customer sets and totals with independent logic before revising a visual.
18. **How protect customer trust?** Relevant offers, transparent contact policy, opt-out/complaint guardrails, access controls, minimization and human review. A high score does not authorize contact.

## Digital business case: 100,000 eligible customers, 10% payroll adoption

**Define:** The stated 10% means 10,000 current adopters under the case assumption. Specify incremental first salary receipt within an agreed window and persistence; do not confuse current ownership with response.

**Understand:** Reconcile eligible nonowners, baseline conversion without contact, digital activity and salary-switch friction. Inspect the current-account → regular-activity → payroll funnel and possible channel barriers.

**Segment:** Start with interpretable engagement/current-account groups and legitimate needs. Exclude current owners, restricted contacts and recent similar campaigns. Treat profile segments as snapshot descriptors.

**Design:** Compare a clear payroll-switch message/onboarding offer with an appropriate control. Choose one primary metric, a practical minimum effect, sample size, allocation, duration, holdout and no-peeking policy before launch.

**Measure:** ITT conversion difference and confidence interval; check SRM, tracking, cost, complaints, unsubscribe and satisfaction. Examine persistence after the main window rather than declaring success on a single click.

**Decide:** Scale only with adequate evidence and acceptable guardrails/economics; otherwise stop, extend under the predefined plan or redesign. The project's payroll simulation is below its planned sample, so it does not justify real rollout.

## Digital business case: travel activation improves but complaints rise

Clarify whether the proposed feature actually helps the eligible traveler. Validate assignment/exposure and complaint denominators, compare absolute differences and business thresholds, stop on a predefined guardrail, inspect message/channel friction and propose a safer retest. Do not trade customer harm for a short-term conversion headline. This matches the simulated Travel stop decision.

## Digital business case: high value, low engagement

Validate the value proxy and missingness, understand customer needs, distinguish product ownership from active use, and compare a relevant feature reminder with no contact. Use incremental meaningful activity/retention and complaint guardrails. Do not label every inactive customer as churn or imply that a score estimates individual causal uplift.

## Questions to ask the interviewer

- How does your team define meaningful engagement and primary banking?
- Who owns salary-domiciliation and campaign KPI definitions?
- How do Digital, Marketing, Product, Data and Compliance share decisions?
- How do you preserve experiment holdouts and prevent contact contamination?
- What customer-outcome data and consent history are available?
- How mature is the Customer 360 and its point-in-time history?
- Which platforms support analysis and BI, and where are their current limits?
- What evidence would make an experiment ready to scale?
- How is Power BI used in recurring business decisions?
- What would success in the first three months of this role look like?
