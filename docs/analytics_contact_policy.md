# Contact policy and experiment-aware opportunities

The policy caps contacts at two in seven days and six in 30 days, requires a three-day gap for similar campaign types, respects the synthetic contact-permission flag and excludes a stable 5% persistent holdout. All counts use only campaign dates before the decision cutoff. Current ownership and eligibility remain hard gates. This is simulation policy, not a real consent system.

The existing model/rule candidate scores are reused. Unvalidated candidates receive a configured discount. A quality-checked, sufficiently powered positive simulated experiment supplies an evidence multiplier only for customers in that experiment's original eligible population who still pass current action eligibility; it is not generalized automatically to every customer. A tested guardrail stop blocks that candidate pending review. No eligible compliant candidate means No Action.

`action_priority_score = existing normalized candidate score × evidence multiplier × contact availability`. Priority is 100 times that index. Existing candidates already incorporate their documented eligibility/business weights. The multiplier is at most one: validated positive lift divided by a configurable scale (capped), versus a cautious fallback for unvalidated actions. This is an explainable **ranking index**, not expected incremental revenue or an individual causal probability. The reported `experimental_lift` is an average simulated trial effect; it is null when unavailable.

Propensity/risk fields keep their original meanings. Digital re-engagement uses inactivity risk as an opportunity component, not a prediction that a campaign will cause a conversion. Current value is an index, not CLV. Value/opportunity quadrants retain an Unknown/Not Applicable group rather than forcing missing inputs into low-value categories. Reason codes explain contact/holdout exclusion, lack of action, cautious propensity-only use or supported simulated experiment evidence. Customer-level access remains restricted.
