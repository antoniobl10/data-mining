# Business decisions and limits

**SYNTHETIC DEMO MODEL.** Every model supports a proposed decision, not an automated customer intervention.

| Use case | Consumer and cadence | Target/action | Offline model metrics | Eventual business metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign | Marketing analyst, per launch | Conversion within 30 days of a sent campaign; rank eligible audience | PR-AUC, lift/gain at capacity, calibration | Campaign conversion and cost per conversion |
| Synthetic churn | Relationship analyst, monthly | No digital event in next 30 days; review re-engagement queue | Recall@top5/top10, PR-AUC, lift | Retention and meaningful reactivation, once actual exit is defined |
| Payroll | Relationship analyst, monthly | First payroll start in next 30 days among no-payroll current-account holders | PR-AUC and lift@10 | Salary domiciliation conversion |
| Travel | Service analyst, monthly | Travel activation in next 30 days among known eligible offered customers without prior activation | PR-AUC, lift, Brier | Service activation rate |
| Next Best Action | Authorized analyst, per batch | Highest eligible weighted candidate, or No Action | Eligibility correctness and transparent ranking | Response, benefit, customer harm and opt-out outcomes from experiments |

Churn is a synthetic inactivity proxy, not account closure or verified attrition. Product starts, payroll and service eligibility are not perfectly causally consistent in the previous generator. The ML layer does not silently repair or manufacture history. The limited historical offers constrain service training and the final current-account requirement constrains payroll. This reconstruction is a demonstration, not a validated event-sourced bank ledger.

The targeting efficiency simulation uses the fixed configured budget and standardized contact cost. Random audience conversions are the expected count under uniform selection; rule-based (engagement) and ML audiences use observed outcomes among selected, historically contacted rows. Contact counts and standardized costs are equal. Conversion differences are retrospective selection estimates, not incremental causal effects. Small positives produce volatile top1/top5 metrics. Repeated customers and tied scores limit uncertainty claims; fixed stable ordering breaks ties and can make a constant model's observed decile lift differ from 1. Constant scores carry no ranking information. Risk flags use stored validation thresholds and may exceed capacity when tied; an operational campaign must use a fixed-size tie-randomized queue instead.

Band edges are the validation prediction 20/40/60/80 percentiles and remain fixed at scoring. Values on a boundary use the lower band. Ties can collapse bands. Very High means a relative score category, not a calibrated 80% chance. High KPI flags use the separate validation top10 threshold; a constant baseline always sets its high flag false because it cannot identify a high-propensity subgroup. Missing ineligible propensity is shown as Not Eligible, never zero performance.

Candidate priorities: payroll probability × configured weight; travel probability × engagement factor × weight; inactivity risk × current value factor × weight; campaign response proxy × engagement factor × cross-sell weight; low-satisfaction rule × recovery weight. Eligibility gates apply first. Highest score wins, below-minimum scores become No Action. Current customer value defaults to a neutral 50 index when unknown, solely for business ranking; no financial source value is imputed in Gold. Priority is 0–100 and is not a probability or monetary return. Cross-sell lacks a dedicated product-response label. FX offers are deferred until a separately validated eligible FX model exists.

To assess causal effectiveness, pre-register eligibility, contact policy, minimum detectable effect and a mature outcome window. Randomize high-score eligible customers to treatment/contact vs holdout/control, keep consistent consent exclusions, log assignment, analyze intention-to-treat, and monitor harm/opt-outs. Payroll success would be a difference in new domiciliation rates across randomized arms. No current campaign dataset has valid randomized treatment/control assignments, so uplift modeling is a roadmap item. Propensity predicts conversion; uplift predicts the difference caused by treatment.
