# Journeys, funnels, cohorts and lifecycle methods

**SYNTHETIC DEMO ANALYTICS.** Do not infer customer actions that source timestamps cannot establish.

Sessionization sorts events by customer, channel, device, timestamp and stable event ID. A gap strictly greater than 30 minutes starts a new session; equality remains in the same session. First events have null previous-event gaps. Previous/next fields stay within reconstructed sessions. Original session IDs remain lineage fields. Raw event-to-stage mapping is configured; unknown events remain Unmapped and no retention/application stage is invented from a generic login. A digital `service_activation` event is an activation-event proxy, not proven payroll/service product conversion.

Observed session paths are truncated after eight steps for labels; only the 50 most common paths plus an Other group enter the path mart. Counts/durations still include the full session. Path-customer membership totals are non-additive across paths: the same customer may have multiple journeys. Dated payroll/service/campaign outcomes can be associated with the last completed preceding session within 30 days. This establishes sequence/association, not causality. Date-only outcomes use completed sessions strictly before the date boundary; intraday ordering cannot be recovered.

Campaign funnels use matured launch cohorts and nested sent/opened/clicked/conversion-within-window flags. There are no open/click availability timestamps, so product views cannot be stitched into a chronological campaign funnel. Service funnels use eligible/offered/activated/currently active relationships; generic service views have no service ID and are omitted. Primary Bank Journey is a **cross-sectional readiness intersection**, not longitudinal conversion: recent digital → current account → regular sessions → payroll → product depth → active services → primary-score threshold. Its final intersection can be smaller than the independent Likely Primary Bank segment. Each next-stage count is a subset of the previous stage. Campaign/service observations and distinct customers are stored separately.

Funnel segmentation compares one dimension at a time; it never sums all country/age/value/channel facets into one audience. Small segmented audiences are omitted. Power BI defaults to Campaign/All when no single selector is supplied. A stage's conversion/drop-off is meaningful within that selected funnel and cohort, not across unrelated populations. Current snapshot segments are retrospective descriptors and not asserted to be historical segment values.

Cohorts use first digital activity, first payroll start, first acquisition of each product, first activation of each service, and first campaign conversion. No customer relationship-start date is invented from tenure. The customer-month grid ends at the observed month; unobserved future months are absent, never zero-retention cells. Activity requires a meaningful session after actual cohort entry, including month zero. Digital period retention permits a customer to return after an inactive month; it is not continuous survival. Denominators are cohort membership, not only active customers.

Product retention reconstructs dated ownership of the acquired product at month end. Service deactivation/use histories are absent, so historical **service-state retention is unavailable**; service-adopter digital retention is explicitly distinct. Current balances are not a historical series: `average_balance` is null rather than backfilled. Cohort current value-proxy descriptors are labeled as current snapshot values. Historical product depth and monthly digital engagement are reconstructed from available dates. Tiny cohort cells can be suppressed in scoped measures and must not be interpreted as robust evidence.

Lifecycle rules use point-in-time product starts/depth, recent activity, engagement/decline and prior payroll. New uses first observed product start as a relationship proxy; Dormant uses prolonged login inactivity/no prior login; At Risk uses no recent events or material event decline; other stages capture activation, engagement and depth/payroll relationships. They are transparent configurable rules, not observed bank exits. Historical lifecycle rows do not receive the current ML churn probability; risk joins only at its actual scoring cutoff. Transition counts are customer-month transitions, not unique customers.
